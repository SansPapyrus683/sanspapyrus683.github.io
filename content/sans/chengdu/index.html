little chengdu trip

this summer, both conservemomenta and i had some time to go back to china after our summer internships. there's a lot to say about the quarter system, but school starting in late september is certainly one of its benefits.

we'd already met up in shanghai (both our hometowns) plenty of times, so this time we decided to take a trip down to chengdu and just mess around there for a bit.

unlike with anime expo, a lot of these photos don't warrant, like, an explanation or caption of sorts. a good portion of them is just scenery that i thought was photoworthy, so i'm not sure how much writing will actually be in this blog post.

eh, i'll just put them here and see where the winds take me.

day 1

our flight was around 3 hours and we took off around 9, so we had the better part of an afternoon to mess around the city.

when i told my my paternal grandfather i was going to go to chengdu, he really shilled this one place called 武侯祠 (wu hou ci), which is a shrine dedicated to zhu geliang. for those of you around the world (as mulaney would say), zhu was a really renowned strategist during the three kingdom period right after the han dynasty. this is despite the fact that the kingdom he worked for, the shu, literally lost. imagine losing a war and still getting glazed to hell and back.

we dropped off our stuff at the hotel and then called an uber there. there was a rock at the entrance gate, and i got conserve to take this photo of me:

the rock reads "sacred ground of the three kingdoms"...i think? my chinese highk sucks so

we then bought tickets and went in. even though china has an official student id of sorts that's issued to college kids nationwide, i still managed to get a student discount of 50% by showing them my ucla bruincard. conserve, the absolute buffoon that he is, forgot to bring his school's student id, so he had to pay full price.

and that was basically all of 武侯祠. pretty good park, pretty mid historical museum.

besides it was also an "ancient town", which these days in china means a glorified shopping mall with vendors shilling their stuff left and right.

i didn't go in expecting to buy anything, but then i came across this stall:

this lowk mogs like over half of the artist alley booths at ax

this booth had anime art that was done using paper-cutting, a classic chinese art form. they had tons of merch, including, unfortunately, arknights, which meant i had to buy a couple. the frames in front were 55 rmb a piece, so thankfully i didn't bleed too much money.

i got a kal'tsit cutout and a goldenglow one, so 110 rmb total.
defo worth it imo, esp given that this kind of art certainly doesn't sell in america

the guy making all of these even took commissions where you could give him a photo and he'd recreate it with paper. conserve decided to get a 500 rmb one using a family group photo. it sounds expensive at first, but then you realize that in the us art commissions can easily go above 100 usd, so really conserve was the one ripping off the artist here with his us purchasing power.

anime art aside, there wasn't anything else notable about this "ancient town".

for dinner i wanted to try hot pot. nearly everyone on both sides of my family doesn't really like hot pot, but according to conserve chengdu is known for it and i wanted to give it another shot, so conserve and i went to this place called 吼堂老火锅 (houtang old hot pot is the best tl i can think of rn for the name):

i couldn't name the meats on this table for the life of me. we got a package deal, so the dishes were pretty hit or miss.

the best i can say is that they met expectations. my opinion on hot pot remains slightly negative, since i never really found the whole workflow of dipping meat into the soup for a couple seconds particularly rewarding or fun. i'll stick with my rice and family-style dishes, thank you very much.

they also had this guy performing face changing for some reason???

都江堰 (du jiang yan)
